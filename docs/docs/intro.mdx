# Dusklapse

Dusklapse ramps a camera's exposure through a sunrise or a sunset, so the finished timelapse
does not step from frame to frame as the light changes. It runs on an iPhone or iPad and talks
to the camera over its own Wi-Fi.

## What it does

- **Measures every frame.** Each JPEG preview is decoded and reduced to one brightness number on
  a 0–10000 scale, where mid-grey sits near 5000. The measurement is a log-average of linear
  luminance, so a bright sky or a dark foreground does not drag it around the way an arithmetic
  mean would.
- **Corrects the exposure between frames.** When a frame falls below the reference, one dial moves
  by one notch - shutter first, then aperture, then ISO at sunset, and the reverse at sunrise.
  Each dial has its own limit and its own on/off switch.
- **Follows the sky, optionally.** Given a position and the time, Dusklapse works out where the
  sun is and walks the reference down as it sets, so night frames read as night rather than as an
  evenly lit day that happens to contain stars.
- **Shows what it is doing.** A histogram of the current frame, and charts of how the dials and
  the brightness have moved across the whole sequence.

## What it does not do

**Dusklapse does not release the shutter.** Frame timing belongs to an external intervalometer,
and the app only adjusts the exposure between frames. This is deliberate: an app that must stay
awake and on schedule for two hours is a fragile thing to hang a night's work on.

It also never transfers RAW files. A frame's file type is checked before anything crosses the
network, and only the JPEG is fetched - set the camera to RAW + Basic and the small JPEG becomes
the preview while the RAW stays on the card.

## What you need

- A camera that exposes its settings over Wi-Fi (see [Cameras](#cameras)).
- An external intervalometer, or the camera's built-in one.
- An iPhone or iPad on the camera's Wi-Fi.

## Cameras

| Vendor | Protocol | Status                                                   |
| ------ | -------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Nikon  | PTP-IP   | Works. Developed against a Z 6 in access-point mode.     |
| Canon  | CCAPI    | Implemented, not yet verified against a body.            |
| Sony   | -        | Not supported.                                           |
| Mock   | -        | A simulated camera, for trying the app without hardware. |

On Nikon bodies the camera must be in **access-point mode** - the one the menu calls _connect to
smart device_. Station mode, where the camera joins an existing network, closes the connection as
soon as the shutter fires.

## Status

Dusklapse is early. It is distributed through TestFlight, the version numbers are still `0.1.x`,
and the parts that have run through a real sunset are the Nikon connection, the measurement and
the ramp. Bug reports are welcome on
[GitHub](https://github.com/gr3enk/dusklapse/issues).
